## Design Context

### Users
- Primary users are peer developers and technically literate readers.
- The core job is reading articles efficiently, discovering useful posts, and building trust in the author's personal technical brand.
- The experience should still feel like a consumer-facing content product, not an admin console or demo page.

### Brand Personality
- Personality: personal, credible, refined.
- The interface should feel trustworthy, readable, and mildly expressive rather than flashy or performative.
- It should support long-form reading and personal identity without overwhelming the content.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Preferred direction: light-brand personal blog, closer to a mature independent blog than a dashboard.
- Keep some personality, but avoid obvious "card pile" composition and avoid a backend-management feel.
- Current neon cyber aesthetic should not dominate future decisions; content, hierarchy, and reading comfort come first.

### Design Principles
- Reading first: prioritize typography, hierarchy, and scanning over decorative chrome.
- Personal, not dashboard: pages should feel like a polished personal publishing product, not a control panel.
- Fewer containers, stronger structure: reduce nested cards and rely more on spacing, rhythm, and grouping.
- Expressive restraint: keep personality through tone and detail, not through heavy visual effects.
- Mobile is primary enough to matter: layouts must adapt cleanly for touch and narrow screens without hiding core actions.
